Step 1 · Team rating

RA = D log10 ( 1 |A| ∑ i∈A 10 riD )

A logarithmic mean, not an arithmetic one. One strong player pulls the team rating above the average, which is why a ringer cannot be hidden behind a weak partner.

Step 2 · Expected point share

EA = 1 1 + 10 RB − RA D

A logistic curve on the rating gap. At RA−RB=D team A is expected to take 1011 of the points.

Step 3 · Actual point share

SA = pA pA + pB

The point ratio counts, not the win. 10 : 9 barely moves anything, 10 : 0 moves a lot.

Step 4 · Raw change

Δ = k ⁢ ( pA + pB ) ⁢ ( SA − EA )

Scaled by total points, so a long game carries more weight than a short one.

Step 5 · Per player

δi = f + ci ⁢ σi ⁢ Δ | Ti |

σi=+1 for team A and −1 for team B; |Ti| is the size of that player's team. Everyone collects the participation bonus f, win or lose.

Step 6 · Placement damping

ci = { 1 if  gi < m min gj < m gjm otherwise

Players still inside their placement period take the full change; everyone else is damped by the least established player in the match. That is what stops people from farming Elo off newcomers. A 2 v 2 game counts as half a match per player, so gi grows by 1/|Ti|.

Why combining is never exact

The participation bonus is paid per entry, not per game played. Three games hand everyone 3⁢f=1.5 Elo for free, a single entry only f. That gap of (n−1)⁢f hits every player equally and no score can make up for it, so it is reported separately in the result.
